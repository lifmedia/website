The New Denny's in Moncton: Photographing a Restaurant Before It Opens

By Jorge Melgar September 4, 2026 7 min read

The parking lot at 70 Queensway Drive had been paved so recently the lines still looked wet. No cars yet. A "Kids Eat Free" sandwich board leaning by the door, a red tower catching full July sun against a blue sky, and a camera on a tripod in the middle of the accessible spaces because for one more week, nobody needed them.

This was the new Denny's in Moncton, a few days before it opened. We were there to photograph the building while it still looked like a rendering — before the first pancake, the first coffee refill, the first Sunday rush.

A New Denny's in Moncton's Mapleton Area

If you drive the Trans-Canada past Mapleton Road, you have seen it. Denny's Moncton sits at 70 Queensway Drive, just off the highway on the Mapleton side of the city. It opened in July 2026 as the brand's first location in Moncton and its second in New Brunswick, owned and operated by Fens Market Group under owner Dwight Fraser.

The building itself is a departure from the diner most of us picture. Dark stacked stone, warm cedar cladding, black steel trim, and that red tower with the yellow Denny's logo you can read from the highway. Inside, it is the "Canada's Modern Diner" look: red booths, blue counter stools, pendant lights over a long counter, and a Moncton mural on the wall. It is open seven days a week until 11 pm, serving all-day breakfast, lunch, dinner, and late-night, with the weekly promotions Denny's Canada runs across the country: Kids Eat Free on Tuesdays and Sundays, Family Night Wednesdays, and 55+ Thursdays.

We think Moncton is in for a treat. It is the kind of place you go with friends after a late game, with family on a Saturday morning, or by yourself with a laptop at 2 pm on a Tuesday. It does not matter what time of day it is, and that is the whole point of a Denny's.

Photographing a Restaurant Before It Opens

There is a short window in the life of every restaurant when the building is finished, the signage is up, and the public has not arrived. It lasts about a week. After that, there are always cars in the lot, always a delivery truck at the side door, always a chair pulled out at an angle. The pre-opening week is the only time the building photographs exactly the way the architect drew it, and it never comes back.

That window is why we were on Queensway Drive in the middle of the day instead of at golden hour. A building like this one is a study in colour: red panel, yellow logo, grey stone, orange cedar, black trim. Flat overcast light would have muddied all of it. Direct sun at midday put hard, clean edges on the stone, made the red tower glow against the sky, and threw the cedar into warm relief. The shadows are honest. On a building with this much material contrast, honest is what you want.

The rest is patience and small decisions:

  • Shoot the sign from the road first. The red tower exists so people on the Trans-Canada can find the exit. The photo that matters most is the one that looks the way a driver sees it.
  • Let the clouds do the work. A big cirrus streak across an otherwise clear sky is free production value. We waited for it to sit above the tower rather than behind it.
  • Get the empty lot. Fresh asphalt, fresh paint, fresh accessible symbols. In two weeks this view will not exist.
  • Read the interior for its designed moments. The counter with the pendant lights. The mural. The red booth against the blue wall. Every new restaurant has three or four views the designer intended, and they deserve to be captured before the first coffee ring lands on the counter.
  • Do not hide the details that say "new". The sandwich board, the tags still on the sign, a protective wrap not yet peeled. A month from now those details are the proof of when the photo was taken.
A restaurant photographs like new exactly once. Everything after that is a restaurant with a history. Both are worth shooting, but only one of them has a deadline.

Why Opening Week Is the Best Marketing Moment a Restaurant Gets

A new location generates more attention in its first month than in the following twelve combined. The press release goes out. The brand's national social accounts post about it. Local news picks it up. People who have never searched for the restaurant before search for it now, because they drove past the red tower and wondered when it opens.

All of that attention lands on a handful of surfaces: the Google Business Profile, the Facebook and Instagram posts, the local listing on the brand's website, and whatever the news outlet grabs to illustrate the story. If those surfaces are carrying phone snapshots taken through a windshield, the restaurant has spent its most valuable month looking smaller than it is.

Real opening photography does a few jobs at once:

  1. It fills the Google Business Profile properly. Exterior, interior, counter, seating. This is where "Denny's near me" searches end up, and profiles with strong photos consistently earn more direction requests and calls.
  2. It gives the franchisee and the brand something to post that matches the national standard. A local opening announcement should look as good as the ones from Toronto or Vancouver. Usually it does not, because nobody planned for it.
  3. It becomes the permanent library. Job postings, the local page on the corporate site, the print menu, the anniversary post a year from now. The opening-week set gets used for years.
  4. It sets up the video. Once the restaurant is running, the next piece is short-form video: the breakfast rush, the staff, the food landing on the table. The stills tell people where you are. The video tells them what it feels like inside.

Restaurant Photography and Video in Moncton, Dieppe and Riverview

We are based in Moncton and we work with restaurants and hospitality businesses across Greater Moncton, Dieppe, Riverview, Fredericton, Saint John, and the rest of Atlantic Canada. Most of that work falls into three buckets:

  • Opening and architectural photography. The exterior and interior set described above, timed to the pre-opening window. This is the piece most restaurants skip and regret.
  • Food and menu photography. Styled, lit, and shot in the restaurant with the kitchen's own plating, so the photos look like what actually arrives at the table.
  • Short-form and cinematic video. Vertical clips for Instagram, TikTok, and Facebook, plus a brand piece when the restaurant has a story worth telling at length.

For a franchise location, the brand usually supplies the national creative. What it does not supply is a photo of this building, on this road, in this city. That is the gap a local crew fills, and it is the gap that shows up in local search.

Questions Restaurant Owners Ask Us

When should we book photography for a new restaurant?

Once the signage is installed and the exterior is clean, and before the soft opening. That is typically a window of five to ten days. Book it when you have the sign install date, not the opening date.

What time of day is best for exterior restaurant photos?

It depends on which way the building faces and what it is made of. Golden hour is the default answer, but a high-contrast façade like the Denny's on Queensway Drive photographs best in strong direct sun. We scout the orientation first and pick the time from that.

Do you photograph the food too?

Yes. Food photography is usually a separate session once the kitchen is running at full speed, because the plates need to come out the way they will for a guest. We handle both, and we can plan them together.

We are a franchise. Do we need local photography if head office sends assets?

Almost always. National assets are for the brand. Local photos are for the Google Business Profile, the local news story, the community Facebook group, and the hiring post. Those are the surfaces that bring people through your door, not the brand's.

Do you work outside Moncton?

Yes, across New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island, and Nova Scotia, in English, French, or Spanish.

Congratulations to Dwight Fraser and the Fens Market Group team on bringing Denny's to Moncton. The city has been waiting for a place that is open when everything else is closed, and now it has one at 70 Queensway Drive.

If you are opening a restaurant, café, or hospitality business anywhere in Atlantic Canada and you want it photographed before it stops looking brand new: get in touch. We will scout the light, plan the window, and leave you with a library you will still be using at the five-year mark.
